About Me

My love for all things racing started when I was in high school. Prior to that, I had been to a few Indy 500's, but my recollection of those races was sitting in a grandstand, with a ton of people, watching the tops of helmets fly by as they went down the front straight. As a child, you couldn’t see the cars, catch the action, or feel the excitement.

When I was in high school, that changed. My dad always watched IndyCar. During my junior/senior year, he took that interest to another level and became a Technical Observer with CART. He worked five or six races that summer, and he took my brother and me along with him.

It was at that same time that I discovered my love of photography. My dad had a Canon AE1 that he bought a few years prior. He would get me a few rolls of film and let me use the camera on those weekends to capture the action. It was thrilling getting my pictures back from the lab, then taking them to the next race to get them autographed by the drivers that I looked up to.

I still have many of those pictures of the all-time greats, drivers like Al Unser, Rick Mears, Johnny Rutherford, etc.

The following year, with a slightly doctored birth certificate (you had to be eighteen years old to be a technical observer and I was only seventeen when I graduated high school), I started working the races with my dad. For the next three years I watched the races from the pit lane, recording information about each pit stop and watching for rule violations.

The most vivid memory of those days was calling a rule violation, driving over equipment, on Arie Luyendyk while he was leading the Michigan 500. Dick Simon scowled at me every time he saw me from that day on. I wouldn't trade those three years for anything in the world.

My Experience Today

Now, as an over-the-hill adult, I continue to have a passion for racing. My interests have strayed from IndyCar into the world of historic and sports car racing. I still have a love of photography, and I continue to capture the action at every race I attend.

My photography is a little different from most on race day. While many photographers parade around with their expensive “white” lenses, I enjoy the challenge of using vintage digital equipment. None of the cameras I own were manufactured after 2013. Most of the cameras and lenses I use cost me less than \$100 on the used market.

I think it's kind of fitting to photograph historic race cars with older camera gear. Recently I have started playing with film again, and I still have that original Canon AE1 that started it all. It won't be long until I get it out to the track again and take everything full-circle.

This site is a place for me to share my photography. It's not pro-level stuff, but I think it's pretty decent. If you see something you like, download it. If you see something that you want to use commercially, just let me know and give me credit.

If you drive any of these amazing machines, and you would like me to do a little photography for you at Road Atlanta, VIR, or Barber Motorsports Park, let me know. I am not a professional, so I am not looking to make money at this, but I wouldn't mind a credential to a race or two.
